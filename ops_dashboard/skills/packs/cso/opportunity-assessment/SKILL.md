# Opportunity Assessment

A structured vet of one opportunity, ending in a written verdict. The verdict
is one of BUILD, TEST FIRST (a two-week experiment), HOLD, or KILL. Never
"looks promising" without a number attached.

## When to use

- A new product idea, a new market, a new vertical, or a large feature bet.
- Someone asks "is this worth building?" before any code is written.
- A partner, an agency or a competitor pitch needs a fair hearing.

Not for: small reversible experiments (just run it), or features already
inside a product that has paying users (use a launch plan instead).

## Step 1 - State the hypothesis in one sentence

Fill every slot or the assessment is not finished:

> For [segment], who [pain], our [product] delivers [outcome] that
> [alternative] does not, and they will pay [price].

If a slot cannot be filled with something observable, that is the finding.
An empty "price" slot usually means there is no willingness to pay.

## Step 2 - Size it from the bottom, never from the top

Top-down market reports ("a $40B market growing 12%") are not evidence.

- Reachable buyers = segment size x realistic reachable share. For a founder
  with an existing audience, reachable share is 0.1% to 1% of the segment, not
  10%: an audience of 2,000 reaching 1% of a 100,000-person segment is 1,000
  people, and that is the number to plan against.
- Bottom-up year-one revenue = reachable buyers x realistic attach rate x
  price. Use an attach rate of 1% to 5% for a first-year plan, 10% only with
  channel access you can name.
- Compare the result to a floor the founder states before the analysis: the
  monthly revenue that makes the work worth it. If the bottom-up number is
  under the floor, the idea does not need more analysis, it needs a decision.

## Step 3 - Demand evidence, ranked

Only the top three tiers count as demand:

1. Someone is already paying for the alternative (a competitor's revenue, a
   service someone already subscribes to).
2. Five or more people asked for it unprompted, in writing, with context.
3. A paid pilot, a preorder, or a letter of intent with a number attached.

Evidence rule: require at least two of tiers 1 to 3, or one paid pilot worth
at least 10% of the year-one floor. Tiers 4 and 5 (waitlist signups, likes,
mentions) are multipliers on tiers 1 to 3, never a substitute: a waitlist of
300 names is not demand proof, it is a distribution list.

## Step 4 - The alternatives, including the real one

Name every way the segment solves this today: the paid tool, the free tool,
the spreadsheet, the agency, the freelancer, and "do nothing and accept the
pain". Price each one if you can. If the alternative set contains "do nothing"
and nothing else, there is no market yet, only a hypothesis.

## Step 5 - Edge and effort, estimated in hours

- Edge: what do you have that a competitor starting today does not? (access,
  data, an audience, a niche, a cost advantage, a channel). No edge means
  you are a feature, not a product.
- Effort: build hours, first revenue in weeks, and the ongoing maintenance
  hours per month. Maintenance is what kills small products; if steady-state
  maintenance exceeds 4 hours a month, price the work accordingly.

## Step 6 - Kill criteria, written before the verdict

Write the conditions that would end it, so later enthusiasm can be checked
against a rule instead of argued about:

- KILL if no paid pilot within 45 days of starting.
- KILL if the build exceeds 40 founder hours before first revenue.
- KILL if the edge depends on a platform or policy you do not control.
- KILL if steady-state maintenance exceeds 4 hours a month at a price the
  segment will pay.
- HOLD if the segment is real but the channel is missing; revisit when a
  channel appears.

## What a good answer looks like

A one-page memo:

- Hypothesis (one sentence, all slots filled).
- Bottom-up size with the three numbers and their sources.
- Demand evidence: what tier, how many, from where.
- Alternatives table: option, price, why it loses.
- Edge, effort, maintenance hours.
- Verdict in the first line: BUILD / TEST FIRST / HOLD / KILL.
- Kill criteria with dates.
- The single next action, under 2 hours to complete.

Every number carries a source: measured, cited, or labelled ASSUMPTION. If a
number cannot be sourced, write ASSUMPTION in front of it and move on.

## Common traps

- Quoting a market size that no buyer would ever be inside.
- Treat engagement as demand, or size from the total audience instead of the
  reachable slice.
- Forget the "do nothing" alternative, which wins most comparisons.
- Write the verdict before collecting the evidence, then hunting support.
- Sizing without maintenance cost, so the product looks better than it is.
