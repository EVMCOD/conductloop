# Launch Plan

A launch with phases, an artifact per phase, and a go/no-go criterion. Ends
with a dated plan and the metric that decides whether it worked.

## When to use

- A new product, a major version, or a price change goes public.
- A feature that a customer was promised, and the promise has a date.
- Re-launching something that flopped the first time.
- A marketplace listing or an app store submission.

Not for: a weekly content post (use a content brief), or a soft announcement
with no date and no commitment.

## Step 1 - Define the launch in one paragraph

Before any channel is picked, write down:

- What is launching, in one sentence a customer would repeat.
- Who it is for, in one segment, not "everyone".
- The single number that will move, and its current value (the baseline):
  signups, MRR, active users, or trials started. One number.
- The date it goes out, and the date the decision is made.

If the baseline is unknown, measure it before launch, not after. A launch with
no baseline cannot be judged.

## Step 2 - Work the four phases

Each phase has an artifact and a go/no-go. Do not skip ahead.

### Phase 1 - Readiness (T-7 to T-1)

Artifact: the launch checklist, one page, every item with an owner. Go/no-go:
all of these are true, or the date moves.

- The landing page works on a phone, loads in under 3 seconds, and has one
  call to action.
- The signup or checkout path is tested end to end with a real card and a real
  cancel.
- Pricing is displayed and matches what will be charged, including tax.
- Support has a written answer for the top 5 questions, including "what
  happens to my data" and "can I cancel".
- Analytics record the funnel: visit, signup, activation, paid.
- One person has run the whole path on their own device, not a laptop.

### Phase 2 - Seeding (T-3 to T-1)

Artifact: the list of who gets told first, and when. Go/no-go: at least 10
named people or 2 channels have had early access, and at least 3 responded
with something other than "looks great". If nobody objected, it was not
tested.

### Phase 3 - Launch (T0)

Artifact: the launch posts, ready to publish, with the URLs already live.
Go/no-go: the first 30 minutes check the site is up, signup works, analytics
fire, and support is watching. If signup fails in the first hour, fix it
before posting again.

### Phase 4 - Review (T+7 and T+30)

Artifact: a one-page readout with the number, the funnel and the decision.
Go/no-go at T+7: if signups are under half the target, the problem is reach
or the offer, not the product, so change one and relaunch. At T+30: compare
against the target, not against the launch-day spike.

## Step 3 - Set the target before the launch

Use a number from the base rate, not a wish:

- Existing audience: expect 2% to 5% to click, and 5% to 15% of clickers to
  convert. Multiply the audience by the lower bound and halve it again for an
  uncertain launch.
- No audience: target qualified conversations, not signups. 10 with the right
  people beats 200 from the wrong ones.
- Price change: the target is revenue at 90 days, with the churn assumption
  stated.

Write the target and its derivation on the checklist. "Spread the word and
see what happens" is not a target.

## Step 4 - Do the follow-up

The launch post is not the launch. 60% of the conversions of a small launch
come from the follow-ups: the people who asked for it and did not convert,
the users who activated and could be asked for a referral, and the people who
said "not now" 90 days ago. Set the follow-up date before the launch.

## What a good answer looks like

One page: the launch in one paragraph; the four phases with their artifacts,
owners and go/no-go criteria; the target number and how it was derived; the
channel list with a date per channel; the follow-up date; and the review
template with its numbers (visits, signups, activations, revenue).

## Common traps

- Launching without a baseline, so the result cannot be judged.
- Treating the launch day as the whole launch; the follow-ups are where most
  of the conversions are.
- Announcing to "everyone" instead of one segment.
- Skipping the readiness phase because "the page is basically done".
- Setting a target by wishing rather than by multiplying.
- Launching on a Friday, or in a week when the founder is unavailable.
- Launching a price change and a new feature in the same week, so neither
  effect can be read.
- Never closing the loop: no T+7 review means the next launch repeats the same
  mistakes.
