# Lead Qualification

A repeatable gate that decides whether a lead is worth a demo, a proposal, or nothing. The point is to spend the founder's scarce hours on leads that
can actually close, and to disqualify fast when they cannot.

## When to use

- A new lead arrives from inbound, a referral, an event, or outreach.
- Before scheduling a demo, a trial extension, or a proposal.
- When too many demos convert to nothing and the funnel is unclear.
- Before promising a roadmap date to a prospect.

Not for: existing paying customers (that is an expansion conversation), and not for support or billing questions on current accounts.

## Step 1 - Apply the checklist (must-have, 3 of 4)

Score each criterion Yes / No / Unknown. A lead is qualified only with at least 3 Yes out of the 4 must-haves, and zero No on the first two.

**Must-have 1 - A real problem, in their words.** The prospect describes a concrete recurring pain, not a desire for a feature. "We spend 6 hours a
week on it" passes; "we would love better reporting" does not.

**Must-have 2 - Someone who can say yes.** A budget holder or a person who influences the decision is on the call. Not only an end user.

**Must-have 3 - A budget.** Money exists for this category, even if the amount is not fixed yet. "We have a tools budget for the team" passes; "we
would need to find money" is a No until it changes.

**Must-have 4 - A deadline.** A reason to act in the next 90 days: a contract renewal, a launch, a board target, a season. "We have been thinking
about it" is not a deadline.

Nice-to-have, scored but never disqualifying: current tool spend above a small threshold, team size above 10, a public signal that the problem is a
priority, an existing tool they are unhappy with, referral from a customer.

## Step 2 - Ask the questions that reveal budget

Ask these in the first call, in this order, and write the answers down verbatim. Asking budget first sounds unpolished; asking it last means the call
was wasted.

1. "What are you doing today to solve this?" (reveals whether money is already moving)
2. "What does that cost you, in time or money per week or month?" (quantifies the problem)
3. "If you solved this, who else would need to approve it?" (finds the decision path)
4. "What have you already budgeted for this in the current period?" (the direct budget question)
5. "What is the date something has to be true by?" (the deadline)
6. "What have you tried and abandoned?" (reveals the real objection and the incumbent)
7. "How would you know this worked, in a number?" (success metric)

Rules:

- Never ask "do you have a budget for this?" It gets a yes or no and teaches the prospect nothing.
- Push past "we would need to look into it": "what would the range be?" and "who would need to approve that number?" A number, or an Unknown, never a
  shrug.
- A budget that exists but is unapproved is Unknown, not Yes. Unknown on a must-have means the lead waits, and you set a date to find out.
- If the answer is "we have no budget because we use a free tool", check the cost of the pain once more before disqualifying. Sometimes the pain is
  real and the trigger is a deadline, not money.

## Step 3 - Disqualify early

Disqualify on the first call when any of these is true. Say it plainly, in the first 10 minutes, and offer a next step that costs you nothing:

- The prospect wants a feature that is not on the roadmap and will not buy the rest of the product.
- No budget path exists and no deadline exists either.
- The decision is at an enterprise procurement level (custom contract, legal review, security review, procurement portal) that a solo-founder company
  cannot service this quarter.
- The prospect needs on-premise, self-hosted or source escrow, and that is not something the business sells.
- The problem is real but the impact is under 1 hour a month and the alternative is free.
- The contact is a student, a hobbyist, or is building the same thing as their day job.
- The competitor is named, chosen, and the switch date is beyond 90 days.
- The account needs support in a language or timezone nobody covers.

Disqualifying is not dropping. The record carries: the disqualifier code, the date, and the trigger that would reopen it ("if they raise the budget
before June" or "when their competitor contract ends in October").

## Step 4 - Decide and record in one line

End every qualification with a written verdict:

- **Qualified** - 3+ must-have Yes, budget named, next step with a date.
- **Nurture** - the problem is real, one criterion is Unknown. Name the one fact to find out, the owner, and the date (within 14 days). A nurture
  record without a date is a lost lead.
- **Disqualified** - code and trigger as above.

Never leave a lead in "we'll see". Every lead leaves the call with one of the three verdicts and a date.

## Step 5 - Read the funnel once a month

- Qualified rate = qualified / first calls. Below 30% means the message or the segment is wrong, not the qualification.
- Qualified to opportunity rate. Below 50% means qualification is too loose; tighten one must-have.
- Opportunity to closed rate. Below 25% means the qualification criteria do not predict willingness to pay, and the checklist needs a new must-have.
- Time spent per qualified lead. If a demo takes 2 hours and the qualified rate is 30%, the real cost per qualified lead is over 6 hours. Count it.
- Disqualification codes, counted. The most common code is the fastest fix available, usually in the message rather than in the checklist.

## What a good answer looks like

- The checklist with Yes / No / Unknown on all 4 must-haves.
- Budget quoted as a number or range, or explicitly Unknown, plus who approves it.
- The deadline as a date.
- One verdict, and for Nurture or Disqualified a trigger and a date.
- A note in the account record that a stranger could read and understand.

## Common traps

- Qualifying on enthusiasm: the prospect says "this is amazing" and has no budget.
- Asking every question except budget.
- Accepting "we would need to look into it" as a budget and carrying the lead for two months.
- Waiting for a demo to discover the deal is unserviceable.
- Disqualifying on company size alone: a 3-person team with a deadline can be a better bet than a 200-person team with a free tool and no urgency.
- Recording "not a fit" without a code, so the same pattern repeats and cannot be counted.
- Leaving a verdict without a date attached.
