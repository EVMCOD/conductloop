# Terms and Policy Drafting

A short document a person can actually read, covering the required sections, with the business customer version handled deliberately rather than by accident.

## When to use

- A product is launching and has no terms or no policy, or the existing one was copied from another product and does not match what it does.
- The product adds a paid plan, a subscription, or an enterprise tier.
- A clause changes (refund window, seat rules, acceptable use) and the published document no longer matches.
- A jurisdiction or market is added and the document needs the local variations.
- A lawyer returns marked-up clauses and the changes need to become readable text.

Not for: a full policy suite across many jurisdictions at once (do the highest-revenue market first), and not for legal advice from this procedure alone.

## Step 1 - Fix the frame before writing

Write these six lines first; every section below depends on them:

- **Who provides the service**: legal entity name, plus registered address if the market requires it.
- **Who the terms cover**: consumers, businesses, or both.
- **What the service is**: 3 sentences, no feature list.
- **Who must accept**: for a business tier, the acceptor binds the company and needs authority.
- **Where the entity is established**: this decides the default governing law and the variants.
- **Version and date**, with a changelog kept alongside the document.

If the product sells to both consumers and businesses, write two documents: consumer terms with the protections that cannot be contracted away, and business terms that can be. Never use the
consumer terms for a business customer, and never the business terms for a consumer.

## Step 2 - Required sections for terms of service

1. **Agreement and acceptance**: who accepts, that acceptance is electronic, and that the acceptor has authority.
2. **The service**: what it does, and the honest limits of what it does.
3. **Eligibility and accounts**: age requirement, one account per person where relevant, security duties, closing.
4. **Your content and licence**: the user keeps ownership, grants the licence to host and process it, and confirms the right to provide it.
5. **Acceptable use**: no unlawful content, no resale, no reverse engineering, no abuse, no infringement.
6. **Payment and subscriptions**: pricing basis, billing cycle, renewal mechanics, cancellation path, effects of cancellation.
7. **Refunds and credits**: the window, the conditions, what is not refundable, the credit regime for failures.
8. **Third-party services**: what the user is responsible for, and that third-party terms apply to them.
9. **Intellectual property**: the platform and its documentation belong to the provider, feedback may be used freely, the user keeps their trademarks.
10. **Confidentiality**: mutual obligations and how long they last.
11. **Warranty disclaimer**: the service is provided as available, with the stated exclusions.
12. **Limitation of liability**: cap of fees paid in the preceding 12 months, no indirect or consequential loss, and carve-outs for confidentiality, data, IP, wilful misconduct, death or injury.
13. **Indemnity**: mutual and limited to third-party claims.
14. **Term and termination**: both parties' rights, notice periods, cure period for breach, what happens to the account and the data.
15. **Governing law and jurisdiction**: law, venue, and any pre-suit escalation step.
16. **Changes to these terms**: how changes are communicated, how far in advance, what to do if not accepted.
17. **Miscellaneous**: assignment, severability, waiver, entire agreement, counterparts, notices, contact route.

## Step 3 - Required sections for a privacy policy

1. **Who we are and how to reach us**, including a data protection contact where the market requires one.
2. **What we collect**: by category, with the source (you, your organisation, devices, third parties), optional items marked.
3. **Why we use it**: the purposes mapped to the lawful basis, with the balancing note for legitimate interest.
4. **Who we share it with**: processors by category, corporate transactions, legal requirements, nothing sensitive.
5. **International transfers**: the destinations and the safeguard used.
6. **Retention**: a table of category and period.
7. **Security**: the measures in plain language, no promise of absolute security.
8. **Your rights**: how to exercise each one, the response deadline, and how to complain to a supervisory authority.
9. **Cookies**: what they do, and how to control them.
10. **Children**: the age the service is intended for, and what happens if a child uses it.
11. **Changes to this policy**, with the last update date and how material changes are communicated.

## Step 4 - Drafting rules for a small product

- **Plain language**: short sentences, one idea each, active voice. If a sentence needs a comma to survive, split it.
- **Concrete over abstract**: "we delete your account data within 30 days of your request" beats "we retain personal data only as necessary".
- **No undefined terms**: define every term used, once, in one place, and no circular promises such as "we may share data with partners as described in our Privacy Policy" inside the privacy
  policy.
- **Every obligation has a consequence**: if the user must act, say what happens if they do not, and by when.
- **Numbers, not adjectives**: 30 days, 24 hours, 12 months. Never "promptly" or "a reasonable period".
- **Readable length**: consumer terms 1,500 to 2,500 words; business terms may run longer, but each section must earn its length.
- **Headings as questions** where the reader is looking for an answer ("Can I get my data back?"), and one document per product.

## Step 5 - What changes when the product sells to businesses

Switch to a business terms set plus a separate order form.

- **Payment and commitment**: seat counts, minimum commitments, annual prepayment, price increase caps at renewal, refund position for prepaid unused time.
- **Service levels**: an uptime target, scheduled maintenance windows, support response times by severity, credits as the exclusive remedy for availability failures.
- **Authority and responsibility**: the customer owns its users' compliance and the content it uploads, and warrants it has the rights to give us the data.
- **Security commitments**: encryption, access control, logging, vulnerability management, breach notification within a stated number of hours.
- **Data processing**: a data processing agreement attached or referenced, sub-processor rules, deletion and export on termination, audit rights.
- **Term and renewal**: an initial term, renewal mechanics, and a stated notice window.
- **Limitation of liability**: a cap expressed as fees paid in the preceding 12 months, with the carve-outs a business buyer needs and no cap on confidentiality, data protection and IP claims.
- **No consumer guarantees**: consumer rights that cannot be contracted away are not written into business terms, and the business terms are not used for consumers.
- **Signature and precedence**: entity name, address, signatory name and title, date, and no signature for a company by someone without authority. The order form overrides the terms for
  commercial terms, the data processing agreement for data processing.

## Step 6 - Review checklist before publishing

Every section from steps 2 or 3 is present, in order. Every clause matches what the product actually does: refund window, cancellation path, data categories, subprocessors, seat rules. No promise
is made that is not backed by an operational capability: if the team cannot answer a data request in 30 days, the policy says what it actually does. Product-specific numbers appear once and are
copied from the running system, not from memory. The effective date and version are set, the changelog records what changed and why, the market variants include only real differences, and a named
person read the document end to end as a customer and as a lawyer.

## What a good answer looks like

The frame: 6 lines fixed before drafting. Two documents when there are two audiences, each with its full section list. A privacy policy whose category list matches the data inventory exactly. A
business terms set plus order form, with the precedence clause. A concrete number for every deadline and every window, a change log and an effective date, and a review date 6 months out.

## Common traps

- Copying terms from a large product and leaving in references to a team, a feature or a jurisdiction that does not apply.
- One document trying to serve consumers and businesses, which drops the consumer protections and breaks the business terms.
- Writing "from time to time we may update these terms" with no notice mechanism.
- Vague promises: "we may share data with third parties" without naming the categories of recipients.
- Promising a deletion deadline in the policy that the backup and search index cannot meet.
- Forgetting the order-form precedence clause, so the order form and the terms conflict silently.
- Publishing with the wrong effective date and no changelog, so nobody can tell what changed.
